# NYC Go-to-Market Analysis: Hyper-Local Domination Strategy

## Market Context

### NYC is not a normal real estate market

- **No centralized MLS.** NYC operates through StreetEasy, REBNY RLS,
  and informal broker networks. No single data feed to auto-sync.
- **Independent brokerages dominate.** Unlike suburban markets with
  Keller Williams / RE/MAX / Coldwell Banker franchises, NYC has
  hundreds of small independent firms (1-20 agents).
- **Storefront windows are prime real estate.** Foot traffic is the
  acquisition channel. A brokerage's window display on a busy avenue
  is their most valuable marketing asset.
- **Agents are brands.** Top NYC agents operate as personal brands,
  not brokerage employees. They care about their listings, their
  reputation, their lead flow.
- **In-person relationships close deals.** This is a handshake market.
  Cold outbound and self-serve SaaS funnels are less effective than
  showing up with a screen under your arm.

### What this means for RePlay

The original landing page analysis assumed a self-serve SaaS motion
with MLS auto-sync as the wedge. NYC inverts that:

- MLS sync is not the hero feature (no MLS to sync from)
- The website doesn't need to convert — deals close in person
- High-touch service is a feature, not a cost
- The product needs a second use case beyond storefront windows

## Two Product Motions

### 1. Storefront Window Displays (Brokerage Sale)

The original pitch: replace printed window cards with a dynamic screen
that always shows current listings. In NYC this is sold brokerage by
brokerage, office by office.

**Buyer:** Broker/owner or office manager
**Pain:** Manually updating window displays, stale sold listings,
can't show video or virtual tours, no walk-in tracking
**Pitch:** "Your storefront works for you 24/7. Current listings,
video tours, QR lead capture — all managed from your phone."
**Close:** In-person demo at their office with a portable screen

### 2. Portable Open House Signage (Agent Sale)

New motion: interactive touch-screen experience that agents bring to
open houses, showings, and events. A portable screen (or tablet on a
stand) running RePlay that showcases the listing with photos, floor
plans, neighborhood info, and captures leads via QR or touch.

**Buyer:** Individual agents (top producers, teams)
**Pain:** Open houses have a sign-in sheet and printed flyers. No
engagement, no follow-up data, no way to showcase the listing beyond
what's physically in the room.
**Pitch:** "Turn every open house into an interactive experience.
Visitors scan, browse, and become leads in your inbox before they
leave the building."
**Close:** Show up at a broker open, demo it live, let them use it
at their next open house

### Why this second motion matters

- **Lower barrier to entry.** One agent, one screen, one open house.
  No brokerage-wide buy-in needed.
- **Viral within brokerages.** Agent uses it at an open house, other
  agents see it, want it. Bottom-up adoption.
- **Recurring touchpoint.** Open houses happen weekly. The product
  is in use constantly, not just sitting in a window.
- **Lead capture is the value prop.** Agents care about leads more
  than brand — every open house visitor who scans becomes a contact.
- **Differentiator.** No competitor is selling portable interactive
  signage for open houses. Window displays are a crowded pitch.

## Hyper-Local Domination Playbook

### Phase 1: Prove It (Months 1-2)

**Goal:** 5 paying customers. Validate both motions. Learn what
resonates and what doesn't.

**Tactics:**
- Pick 2-3 NYC neighborhoods with high brokerage density
  (Upper East Side, Park Slope, Williamsburg, Astoria)
- Walk in with a portable screen running RePlay. Live demo on
  the spot. "Can I show you something for 3 minutes?"
- Offer first month free or heavily discounted for early adopters
- Attend broker opens — demo the portable open house product to
  agents directly
- Ask every prospect: "What would make you tell another broker
  about this?" — find the word-of-mouth trigger

**What you learn:**
- Which motion closes faster (window vs open house)
- What objections come up
- What the right price point feels like
- Whether agents or brokers are the real buyer

### Phase 2: Dominate a Neighborhood (Months 3-4)

**Goal:** Become the known signage provider in 1-2 neighborhoods.
Every brokerage on the block should know your name.

**Tactics:**
- Concentrate on the neighborhood(s) where Phase 1 worked
- Case study from early customers: "XYZ Realty on Madison Ave
  increased walk-in inquiries by X% in their first month"
- Referral incentive: "Refer a brokerage, get a month free"
- Partner with local real estate networking groups and events
- Co-market with early customers: put "Powered by RePlay" on
  their screens with a QR code to your site
- StreetEasy integration or data import as a feature (not MLS
  sync, but "import your StreetEasy listings")

**Network effects:**
- Brokerage A has a screen → walk-by traffic notices → Brokerage B
  across the street asks "what's that?"
- Agent uses portable screen at open house → listing agent at the
  next showing asks about it
- Density creates visibility. One screen per block is invisible.
  Three screens per block is a pattern.

### Phase 3: Expand to Adjacent Neighborhoods (Months 5-8)

**Goal:** 30-50 customers across 5-10 neighborhoods. Repeatable
playbook.

**Tactics:**
- Hire or contract a local sales rep who knows the neighborhoods
- Template the demo flow, objection handling, and onboarding
- Build a "NYC brokerages using RePlay" page on the site
- Introduce tiered pricing (by now you know what the market pays)
- Add features that early customers asked for

### Phase 4: Beyond NYC (Month 9+)

**Goal:** Take the proven playbook to another dense urban market
(Miami, Chicago, LA, Boston).

**Only do this after NYC is locked in.** The playbook should be
documented, pricing validated, churn understood, and unit economics
proven before expanding.

## Website Role in This Strategy

The website is not the conversion engine. In-person demos close deals.
The website serves three purposes:

1. **Credibility check.** After a demo, the prospect Googles you.
   The site needs to look professional and confirm what they heard.
2. **Self-service for warm leads.** "My friend at ABC Realty told me
   about you" — they should be able to see pricing and sign up or
   book a demo without friction.
3. **Open house product page.** Agents who saw the portable screen
   at an open house need a page explaining exactly what they saw
   and how to get it.

### What the site needs

- Clean, professional, fast. Not a conversion-optimized landing page
  with urgency tactics.
- Two clear product sections: Window Displays + Open House Signage
- Real photos of the product in NYC storefronts and at open houses
- Pricing visible (builds trust for a high-touch sale)
- "Book a demo" CTA (primary) + "Start free trial" (secondary)
- NYC-specific messaging: "Built for NYC brokerages"
- Case studies / testimonials as they come in

### What the site does NOT need

- Aggressive conversion optimization
- Complex multi-step funnels
- MLS sync as hero messaging (irrelevant in NYC)
- Feature comparison charts
- Chatbots or live chat

## Pricing Considerations for NYC

NYC brokerages operate differently than suburban offices:

- Higher rent = more cost-sensitive on tools, but also higher
  value per lead (average NYC commission is $30-50K+)
- Independent brokerages have smaller budgets but make faster
  decisions (no corporate approval chain)
- Agents are used to paying for their own tools and marketing

### Suggested starting prices (validate in Phase 1)

| Product | Price | Includes |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Window Display (per screen) | $99/mo | Hardware rental optional, software, support |
| Open House Kit (per agent) | $49/mo | Software license, portable stand optional |
| Brokerage Bundle (3+ screens) | $249/mo | Window + lobby + open house |

Hardware rental or purchase as an add-on. Some brokerages will have
screens; agents almost certainly won't.

## Product Implications

### Features that matter for NYC

- **StreetEasy import** — not MLS sync, but "paste your StreetEasy
  listing URL and we pull the data." Solves the data entry problem
  without requiring a feed.
- **Touch/interactive mode** — for open house portable screens.
  Visitors can swipe through photos, view floor plans, scan QR
  for more info.
- **Lead capture at open house** — QR scan or touch-to-submit form.
  "Enter your email to get the floor plan." Captures leads that
  the sign-in sheet misses.
- **Agent branding** — agents want their photo, name, phone on the
  screen. Personal brand is everything in NYC.
- **Neighborhood content** — transit info, local restaurants, school
  ratings. "Live here" storytelling that static cards can't do.
- **Easy content update from phone** — broker/agent updates a listing
  from their phone, screen updates in real time. No desktop needed.

### Features that DON'T matter for NYC launch

- MLS auto-sync
- Multi-office management (most are single-office)
- White-labeling
- Content scheduling / day-parting
- Complex user roles (it's the broker and maybe an assistant)

## Risks

1. **Hardware logistics.** Selling software is clean. The moment you
   ship or install screens, you become a hardware company with
   support obligations. Mitigate: recommend specific screens,
   offer setup service, but don't own the hardware.

2. **Churn from seasonal agents.** NYC has many part-time or
   seasonal agents. If they churn in winter, unit economics suffer.
   Mitigate: annual pricing discounts, brokerage-level contracts
   over agent-level.

3. **StreetEasy dependency.** If StreetEasy blocks scraping or
   changes their API, the data import feature breaks. Mitigate:
   manual entry as fallback, build direct partnerships.

4. **Small TAM per neighborhood.** A neighborhood might have 20-40
   brokerages. At $99-249/mo each, that's $2K-10K MRR per
   neighborhood. Need density across many neighborhoods to scale.

## Success Metrics

### Phase 1 (prove it)

- 5 paying customers
- Both motions tested (window + open house)
- Average deal closes in < 2 weeks from first demo
- At least 1 unsolicited inbound ("my friend told me about you")

### Phase 2 (dominate)

- 15-20 customers in 1-2 neighborhoods
- 1+ referral per existing customer
- Churn under 5% monthly
- Case study with real metrics

### Phase 3 (expand)

- 30-50 customers across 5-10 neighborhoods
- Repeatable sales playbook documented
- Positive unit economics (CAC < 3 months of revenue)
- Waitlist or inbound from neighborhoods you're not in yet
